# Extracted Historical Claims: Gudimallam Parasurameshwara Linga

Each claim below is atomized, classified by evidence strength, and linked directly to verified bibliographic sources. Conflicting scholarly interpretations are recorded explicitly.

---

## Claim 1: In Situ Erection & Stratigraphic Antiquity (Phase I)
* **Claim ID**: `CLAIM-GUD-01`
* **Claim Type**: `ARCHAEOLOGICAL_STRATIGRAPHY` / `CHRONOLOGY`
* **Statement**: The Gudimallam Linga was originally erected in situ directly on virgin soil within an open-air hypaethral enclosure surrounded by a square brick floor and a stone railing (*vedika*) during Phase I, datable stratigraphically to the 3rd–2nd century BCE.
* **Consensus Status**: `STRONG_CONSENSUS`
* **Evidence Classification**: `STRONG_EVIDENCE`
* **Evidence Typology**: `ARCHAEOLOGICAL`
* **Supporting Evidence**:
  * **EV-GUD-01 (ASI Stratigraphic Trench)**: The 1973–74 sanctum excavation by Dr. I.K. Sarma revealed that the stone railing and Floor 1 were buried intact beneath subsequent floor layers, never disturbed since initial installation.
  * **EV-GUD-02 (Ceramic & Brick Metrology)**: Burnt bricks measuring $42 \times 21 \times 7\text{ cm}$ (standard Mauryan proportions) accompanied by shards of Megalithic Black-and-Red Ware and early historic black-slipped ware in the foundation trench.
* **Citing Sources**:
  * `[SRC-GUD-01]` Sarma, *The Development of Early Saiva Art and Architecture* (1982), pp. 43–58, Plates 10–18.
  * `[SRC-GUD-02]` *Indian Archaeology 1973–74: A Review* (1979), pp. 4–5, Plate II.
* **Analysis**: Provides definitive physical archaeological proof that the linga was not a later medieval insertion into the temple, but the original sacred core around which all subsequent structures evolved.

## Claim 2: Earliest Extant Anthropomorphic Shiva Linga in India
* **Claim ID**: `CLAIM-GUD-02`
* **Claim Type**: `ATTRIBUTION` / `ICONOGRAPHIC_IDENTITY`
* **Statement**: The Gudimallam Linga is the earliest extant stone sculpture in the Indian subcontinent to combine a realistic phallic shaft (*urdhva-linga*) with a two-armed standing anthropomorphic deity (*Rudra/Shiva*).
* **Consensus Status**: `SETTLED_CONSENSUS`
* **Evidence Classification**: `STRONG_EVIDENCE`
* **Evidence Typology**: `ART_HISTORICAL` / `ARCHAEOLOGICAL`
* **Supporting Evidence**:
  * **EV-GUD-03 (Comparative Chronology)**: No earlier dated sculptural linga with anthropomorphic carving has been documented in South Asian archaeology. The earliest Mathura *mukhalingas* (e.g., Bhita linga, Mathura Museum collections) date to the 1st century BCE to 1st century CE, chronologically succeeding or running parallel to Gudimallam.
* **Citing Sources**:
  * `[SRC-GUD-01]` Sarma (1982), pp. 37–42.
  * `[SRC-GUD-05]` Gopinatha Rao (1916), pp. 65–68.
  * `[SRC-GUD-06]` Coomaraswamy (1927), p. 67.
  * `[SRC-GUD-07]` Kramrisch (1981), pp. 117–120.

## Claim 3: Scholarly Debate — Stratigraphic vs. Stylistic Dating
* **Claim ID**: `CLAIM-GUD-03`
* **Claim Type**: `CHRONOLOGY`
* **Statement**: A significant historiographical debate exists regarding whether the sculpture was carved in the 3rd–2nd century BCE (Mauryan/early Satavahana period) or the late 1st century BCE (late Shunga/Andhra period).
* **Consensus Status**: `ONGOING_DEBATE`
* **Evidence Classification**: `SCHOLARLY_DEBATE`
* **Evidence Typology**: `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION`
* **Conflicting Positions**:
  * **Position A (3rd–2nd c. BCE — Supported by I.K. Sarma, ASI)**:
    * *Argument*: Stratigraphically sealed by Phase I brick floor, Megalithic Black-and-Red ware, and early stone railing. The absence of Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) in the lowest layer is typical of contemporary southern Mauryan/post-Ashokan Deccan sites (e.g., Amaravati Period I).
    * *Source*: `[SRC-GUD-01]` Sarma (1982), pp. 63–72.
  * **Position B (Late 1st c. BCE — Supported by Coomaraswamy, Rowland, Sivaramamurti)**:
    * *Argument*: Stylistic comparison of the standing deity's turban, heavy disk ear ornaments (*patra-kundalas*), and the crouching Yaksha below him reveals unmistakable formal similarities with the yaksha reliefs at Bharhut, Bodh Gaya, and Sanchi Stupa II.
    * *Source*: `[SRC-GUD-06]` Coomaraswamy (1927), p. 67; `[SRC-GUD-08]` Sivaramamurti (1976), p. 32.
* **Resolution in System**: The database records `start_year = -300`, `end_year = -50`, with `is_dating_uncertain = true` and `dating_statement = "Phase I: c. 3rd–2nd c. BCE (Sarma) / c. 1st c. BCE (Coomaraswamy)"`.

## Claim 4: Identification of Deity as Vedic Rudra / Agni-Rudra
* **Claim ID**: `CLAIM-GUD-04`
* **Claim Type**: `ICONOGRAPHIC_IDENTITY`
* **Statement**: The anthropomorphic male deity carved in high relief represents an archaic Vedic conception of Rudra (fused with Agni), characterized by the holding of a slain sacrificial ram/goat, a water vessel (*kamandalu*), and a hunter's battle-axe (*parashu*).
* **Consensus Status**: `STRONG_CONSENSUS`
* **Evidence Classification**: `GOOD_EVIDENCE`
* **Evidence Typology**: `ICONOGRAPHIC` / `LITERARY`
* **Supporting Evidence**:
  * **EV-GUD-04 (Vedic Textual Parallels)**: In the *Taittiriya Samhita* and *Satarudriya* (Yajurveda), Rudra is invoked as the lord of the forest, hunter, carrying a weapon (*ayudha*), and closely linked with Agni and animal sacrifice (*pasu*). The absence of typical classical Shaiva attributes (third eye, crescent moon, trishula, bull Nandi) emphasizes its pre-Puranic, early historic iconography.
* **Citing Sources**:
  * `[SRC-GUD-07]` Kramrisch (1981), pp. 120–124.
  * `[SRC-GUD-08]` Sivaramamurti (1976), pp. 31–34.
  * `[SRC-GUD-01]` Sarma (1982), pp. 68–71.

## Claim 5: Medieval Apsidal Stone Reconstruction under Banas & Cholas (Phase III)
* **Claim ID**: `CLAIM-GUD-05`
* **Claim Type**: `ARCHITECTURAL_STRATIGRAPHY` / `PATRONAGE`
* **Statement**: The surviving granite stone temple with its distinct apsidal (*hastiprishta*) sanctum was erected in Phase III between the 9th and 12th centuries CE over the Phase II brick temple, under the patronage of the Bana and Chola dynasties.
* **Consensus Status**: `SETTLED_CONSENSUS`
* **Evidence Classification**: `STRONG_EVIDENCE`
* **Evidence Typology**: `EPIGRAPHIC` / `ARCHITECTURAL`
* **Supporting Evidence**:
  * **EV-GUD-05 (Stone Inscriptions in Temple Walls)**: Numerous donor inscriptions in Tamil and Grantha scripts carved on the basement and pilasters of the temple recording grants by Bana King Vijayaditya II and Chola monarchs Parantaka I (907–955 CE), Rajaraja I (985–1014 CE), and Vikrama Chola (1118–1135 CE).
* **Citing Sources**:
  * `[SRC-GUD-04]` *Epigraphia Indica*, Vol. XI (1911–12), pp. 222–228; Vol. XVII (1923–24), pp. 1–7.
  * `[SRC-GUD-01]` Sarma (1982), pp. 73–85.
