# Research Dossier: Gudimallam Parasurameshwara Linga

## 1. Geographical & Administrative Profile
* **Site Name**: Parasurameshwara Temple Complex (also spelled *Parasuramesvara*)
* **Modern Location**: Gudimallam Village, Yerpedu Mandal, Tirupati District (formerly Chittoor District), Andhra Pradesh, India
* **Topography**: Situated on the eastern plains near the banks of the Suvarnamukhi River, approximately 13 km southeast of Renigunta.
* **Coordinates**: Lat $13^\circ 34' 56''\text{ N}$, Long $79^\circ 35' 05''\text{ E}$ (approx. $13.5822^\circ\text{ N}, 79.5847^\circ\text{ E}$)
* **Administrative Protection**: Monument of National Importance under the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), Hyderabad/Chennai Circle.

---

## 2. Historiographical & Discovery Background
* **Initial Academic Discovery (1903)**:
  * Discovered for modern art-historical scholarship by epigraphist and scholar **T.A. Gopinatha Rao** in 1903.
  * Published in his seminal work *Elements of Hindu Iconography* (Vol. II, Part I, 1916).
  * Gopinatha Rao identified the sculpture as a unique early manifestation of Shiva combining an erect phallus (*linga*) with an anthropomorphic deity (*Rudra*).
* **Early 20th Century Scholarly Interpretations**:
  * **Ananda K. Coomaraswamy** (*History of Indian and Indonesian Art*, 1927) analyzed the sculpture stylistically, noting close affinities of the anthropomorphic figure and the crouching yaksha with the stone reliefs of Bharhut and Bodh Gaya. He assigned it to the 1st century BCE.
  * **Stella Kramrisch** (*The Presence of Siva*, 1981) examined the philosophical and iconographic integration of the Vedic Rudra and the indigenous linga cult.

---

## 3. Archaeological Excavations (1973–1974)
Prior to 1973, scholars debated whether the linga was original to the site or brought from elsewhere, and dated it solely on subjective stylistic criteria. In 1973–74, during conservation work by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), **Dr. Inguva Karthikeya Sarma** (Superintending Archaeologist, Temple Survey Project) conducted systematic stratigraphic excavations inside the *garbhagriha* (sanctum sanctorum).

### Excavation Findings & Structural Sequence:
The excavations reached virgin soil and revealed that the linga had **never been moved from its original foundation**:

1. **Phase I — Open-Air Hypaethral Shrine (c. 3rd–2nd Century BCE)**:
   * The linga was originally set up in an open-air enclosure directly on virgin soil.
   * A stone railing (*vedika*) of two tiers surrounded the linga shaft, composed of stone uprights, crossbars (*suchi*), and coping stones, mirroring the design of Mauryan and Sunga Buddhist railings.
   * Floor 1 was composed of burnt bricks measuring $42 \times 21 \times 7\text{ cm}$ (characteristic of Mauryan/early historic brick metrology).
   * Associated ceramic evidence: Megalithic Black-and-Red Ware, black-slipped ware, and early historic red ware shards.
2. **Phase II — Apsidal Brick Temple (c. 1st–3rd Century CE)**:
   * An apsidal brick structure (*vimana*) was erected over the Phase I stone railing, enclosing the linga within a covered shrine.
   * Construction of Floor 2 (lime plaster over rubble foundation).
   * Associated with Andhra/Satavahana and early Ikshvaku ceramic horizons, including Rouletted Ware shards.
3. **Phase III — Medieval Stone Temple Reconstruction (c. 9th–12th Century CE)**:
   * The apsidal brick temple was replaced by a granite stone temple during the reign of the Bana and Chola dynasties.
   * Sanctum redesigned with an apsidal stone *garbhagriha* (*hastiprishta* / elephant-back plan).
   * The ancient Phase I stone railing and brick floor were buried beneath the sanctum paving stones, serving as the hidden foundation of the linga shaft.

## 4. Object Profile & Iconographic Analysis

### Physical Attributes:
* **Object Name**: Gudimallam Anthropomorphic Shiva Linga
* **Material**: Compact hard dark-brown igneous stone (dolerite / metamorphic igneous rock), quarried from local hills and finely polished.
* **Dimensions**: 
  * Visible height above floor: approx. $1.52\text{ m}$ ($5\text{ ft}$).
  * Total length including underground uncarved shaft: approx. $2.45\text{ m}$ ($8\text{ ft}$).
  * Nut (glans) diameter: approx. $34\text{ cm}$.

### Iconography:
1. **Phallic Form**:
   * The shaft is rendered realistically as an erect phallus (*urdhva-linga*), complete with a longitudinal groove and accurately delineated glans (*nut*).
2. **Anthropomorphic Deity**:
   * Carved in high relief on the anterior face of the shaft.
   * Standing standing firmly (*samabhanga*) with knees slightly outward on the shoulders of a dwarf-like crouching yaksha.
   * **Attributes**:
     * Right hand: Holds a dead ram (or sacrificial goat / antelope) by its hind legs.
     * Left hand: Holds a small spherical pot (*kamandalu* / *amrita-patra*), with a small battle-axe (*parashu*) resting on the left shoulder.
   * **Drapery & Adornments**:
     * Wears a diaphanous lower garment (*dhoti*) secured with an elaborate sash (*katisutra*) tied in front with hanging loops.
     * Large disk-shaped ear ornaments (*patra-kundalas*).
     * Armlets, bangles, and a torque around the neck.
     * Hair arranged in fine braided locks coiled around the head, characteristic of pre-Kushan indigenous sculptural fashion.
3. **Crouching Dwarf (Yaksha / Apasmara)**:
   * Crouching on knees with folded limbs, supporting the deity.
   * Features animal-like pointed ears and grotesque features, typifying early Indian Yaksha representations.

## 5. Historiographical Consensus & Scholarly Debate

* **Primary Consensus**:
  * Unanimous academic recognition that the Gudimallam Linga is the **earliest extant anthropomorphic Shaiva monument in India**.
  * Complete consensus that the linga stands in its original, in situ archaeological findspot.
* **Chronological Disagreement**:
  * **View A (Stratigraphic/Archaeological — I.K. Sarma, ASI)**: Assigns the Phase I erection to the **3rd–2nd century BCE** based on sealed brick dimensions ($42 \times 21 \times 7\text{ cm}$), Black-and-Red ware, and early stone railing stratigraphy.
  * **View B (Art-Historical/Stylistic — Coomaraswamy, Rowland, Sivaramamurti)**: Assigns the sculpture to the **1st century BCE** based on close stylistic parallels with Bharhut Yakshas (e.g., Kubera, Kupiro) and early Satavahana art.
* **Resolution in Bharat Heritage Atlas**:
  * Both views are recorded with full attribution. View A is classified under `STRONG_EVIDENCE` (in situ excavation stratigraphy), and View B under `GOOD_EVIDENCE` / `SCHOLARLY_DEBATE` (comparative art history).
