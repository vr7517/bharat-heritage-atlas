# Research Dossier: Sanchi Great Stupa & Archaeological Complex

## 1. Geographical & Administrative Profile
* **Monument Name**: Great Stupa of Sanchi (Stupa 1) & Buddhist Monumental Complex
* **Site Complex**: Sanchi Archaeological Area (Ancient *Kākanāya*, *Kākanāva*, *Kākanāda-bota*, *Botiya-śrī-parvata*)
* **Modern Location**: Sanchi Town, Raisen District, Madhya Pradesh, India
* **Topography**: Situated upon the crest of an isolated flat-topped sandstone hill (approx. $90\text{ m}$ above the plains), adjacent to the Betwa River valley, $9\text{ km}$ southwest of ancient Vidisha (Besnagar) and $46\text{ km}$ northeast of Bhopal.
* **Coordinates**: Lat $23^\circ 28' 46''\text{ N}$, Long $77^\circ 44' 22''\text{ E}$ ($23.4794^\circ\text{ N}, 77.7394^\circ\text{ E}$)
* **Administrative & Heritage Status**:
  * UNESCO World Heritage Site (Inscribed in 1989; Criteria i, ii, iii, iv, vi; UNESCO Ref: 524).
  * Monument of National Importance under the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), Bhopal Circle.

---

## 2. Historiographical & Discovery History

### 2.1 Early 19th-Century Discovery
* **General Henry Taylor (1818)**:
  * First documented the surviving monuments while on campaign during the Third Anglo-Maratha War.
  * Stupa 1 was found largely intact with three of its four gateways still standing, though overgrown with dense jungle vegetation.
* **Amateur Clearance and Damage (1819–1850)**:
  * Captain Johnson (1822) breached the southwestern side of the Great Stupa down to its core in search of treasure, causing structural collapse of the western gate and part of the dome.

### 2.2 Cunningham and Maisey Excavations (1851)
* **Fieldwork**: Major Alexander Cunningham and Captain F.C. Maisey conducted the first systematic scientific survey and clearing of the Sanchi ruins.
* **Publication**: Alexander Cunningham, *The Bhilsa Topes; or, Buddhist Monuments of Central India* (London: Smith, Elder and Co., 1854).
* **Reliquary Discoveries**:
  * **Stupa 3**: Cunningham excavated the chamber beneath the stupa center and discovered a stone relic chest containing two inscribed steatite/sandstone caskets inscribed with the names of the two chief disciples of the Buddha:
    * *Sāriputasa* (Venerable Śāriputra)
    * *Mahā-Mogalānasa* (Venerable Mahā-Maudgalyāyana)
  * **Stupa 2**: Uncovered relic boxes containing bone fragments of Buddhist teachers of the late Mauryan era (*Haimavata* masters).

### 2.3 Sir John Marshall Excavations & Conservation (1912–1919)
* **Directorate**: Sir John Marshall, Director General of Archaeology in India, assisted by French orientalist Alfred Foucher and engineer Rao Bahadur Daya Ram Sahni.
* **Conservation Work**:
  * Cleared massive debris terraces accumulated over centuries.
  * Rebuilt the collapsed dome sections of Stupa 1.
  * Re-erected the fallen Southern and Western Gateways (*toranas*).
  * Founded the Sanchi Archaeological Museum on site.
* **Authoritative Monograph**:
  * John Marshall and Alfred Foucher, *The Monuments of Sāñchī* (3 Volumes, Calcutta/London: Government of India / Probsthain, 1940), containing complete photographic, epigraphic, and architectural documentation.

## 3. Multi-Phase Architectural Evolution

```
[Phase I: Mauryan / Ashokan] (c. mid-3rd c. BCE)
  └─ Brick core stupa (half current diameter) + Monolithic Ashokan Pillar with 4-Lion capital & Schism Edict.
         │
[Phase II: Shunga Enlargement] (c. mid-2nd c. BCE)
  └─ Sandstone ashlar casing (doubling volume), upper pradakshina patha, harmika, chhatravali, and ground stone vedika.
         │
[Phase III: Satavahana Gateways] (c. 1st c. BCE – early 1st c. CE)
  └─ Four elaborately carved stone toranas (South, North, East, West) with Jataka reliefs and Guild donor epigraphs.
         │
[Phase IV: Gupta Additions] (c. 5th c. CE)
  └─ Four colossal seated Buddha statues placed at the cardinal entrances + Temple 17 (Early Gupta flat-roofed shrine).
```

### 3.1 Phase I: Mauryan / Ashokan Foundation (c. mid-3rd century BCE, c. 250 BCE)
* **Patronage**: Mauryan Emperor Ashoka.
  * According to the *Mahavamsa* (Ch. XIII), Prince Ashoka fell in love with Devi (daughter of a merchant of Vidisha) while stationed at Ujjain. Devi bore him Mahendra and Sanghamitta, who later led the Buddhist mission to Sri Lanka. Sanchi was selected as a prime monastic foundation near Devi's hometown.
* **Structure**:
  * A low, solid hemispherical dome of mud and burnt brick, approximately half the diameter of the present monument (approx. $18\text{ m}$ diameter).
  * Surrounded by a wooden railing and crowned with an elevated umbrella on a small stone terrace.
* **Ashokan Monolithic Pillar (Pillar 25 / Pillar 10)**:
  * Erected immediately beside the Southern entrance.
  * Monolithic shaft of fine-grained Chunar sandstone ($12.8\text{ m}$ / $42\text{ ft}$ high) exhibiting standard Mauryan high polish (*mirror polish*).
  * Crowning capital: Inverted bell lotus surmounted by an abacus with birds and a four-lion back-to-back group (closely related to the Sarnath capital).
  * **Schism Edict (*Sanghabheda Edict*)**: Inscribed in 20 lines of Brahmi script warning monks and nuns against creating divisions in the Sangha, ordering dissenters to be clothed in white and expelled from the monastery.

### 3.2 Phase II: Shunga Enlargement & Stone Balustrade (c. mid-2nd century BCE, c. 150 BCE)
* **Structural Transformation**:
  * The original brick stupa was completely encased in a thick shell of hammer-dressed yellow-grey sandstone ashlar blocks, doubling its diameter to **$36.6\text{ m}$ ($120\text{ ft}$)** and raising its height to **$16.5\text{ m}$ ($54\text{ ft}$)** (excluding the superstructure).
* **Upper Circumambulatory Path (*Medhi*)**:
  * An elevated terrace ($4.9\text{ m}$ / $16\text{ ft}$ above ground) encircled the dome, accessed by a double flight of stone stairs (*sopana*) on the south side.
* **Superstructure**:
  * Crowning the dome was a heavy square stone railing (*harmika*), enclosing a central shaft (*yasti*) supporting a three-tiered stone umbrella system (*chhatravali*), symbolizing the *Triratna* (Buddha, Dharma, Sangha).
* **Ground Stone Balustrade (*Vedika*)**:
  * A monumental ground railing ($3.3\text{ m}$ / $11\text{ ft}$ high) enclosing a stone-paved lower circumambulatory path (*pradakshina patha*).
  * Constructed without mortar, using joinery techniques adapted directly from carpenter woodwork:
    * Octagonal stone pillars (*thaba*)
    * Horizontal crossbars lens-shaped in cross section (*suchi*)
    * Heavy coping stones (*ushnisha*) with tenon-and-mortise joints
  * Over 600 short Brahmi donative inscriptions carved onto the railing components recording gifts from monks, nuns, merchants, guilds, and lay pilgrims across India.

### 3.3 Phase III: Satavahana Monumental Gateways (*Toranas*, c. 1st century BCE to early 1st century CE)
* **Erection Sequence**:
  * South Gateway (earliest, c. mid-to-late 1st c. BCE)
  * North Gateway (finest preservation, c. late 1st c. BCE)
  * East Gateway (famous for the freestanding Yakshi/Shalabhanjika bracket, c. late 1st c. BCE)
  * West Gateway (c. early 1st c. CE)
* **Architectural Form**:
  * Each gateway rises to approx. $10.5\text{ m}$ ($34\text{ ft}$) in height and consists of two massive square upright pillars supporting three horizontal architraves separated by square blocks and figural statues.
* **Narrative Iconography**:
  * **Aniconic Buddha Representation**: The Buddha is never depicted in human form on the gateways, but represented exclusively through sacred symbols:
    * Footprints (*Buddhapaduka*)
    * Wheel of the Law (*Dharmachakra*)
    * Empty Throne beneath the Bodhi tree
    * Umbrella (*Chhatra*)
    * Stupa (*Parinirvana*)
  * **Jataka Tales**: Complete narrative friezes illustrating the previous lives of the Buddha:
    * *Vessantara Jataka* (supreme generosity)
    * *Chaddanta Jataka* (six-tusked elephant)
    * *Mahakapi Jataka* (monkey king self-sacrifice)
    * *Sama Jataka* (devoted filial son)
  * **Historical Episodes**: Ashoka's visit to the Bodhi Tree at Bodh Gaya; the War of the Relics over the distribution of the Buddha's physical remains.
* **Vidisha Ivory Carvers Inscription**:
  * Carved on the Southern Gateway architrave:
    ```text
    Vedisakehi damtakārehi rūpakammaṃ kataṃ
    ```
  * Translation: *"Done by the ivory carvers (dantakāra) of Vidisha."*
  * Proves that guild craftsmen trained in miniature ivory carving transferred their techniques directly into fine sandstone relief sculpture.
* **Satavahana Royal Inscription**:
  * Also on the Southern Gateway: records a donation from *Ananda*, the foreman of artisans (*avesanin*) of King Siri Satakarni (*Rāño Siri Sātakaṇisa*).

## 4. Epigraphical Corpus & Linguistic Profile
* **Total Epigraphs**: More than 800 inscriptions discovered across the Sanchi hill.
* **Chronological Span**: From the 3rd century BCE (Ashokan Mauryan) to the 10th–12th century CE (Late Medieval).
* **Paleography & Language**:
  * Brahmi script evolving from early Mauryan monumental to Middle Brahmi (Shunga/Satavahana) and Gupta transitional.
  * Language: Early Middle Indo-Aryan (Epigraphical Prakrit), shifting to Sanskrit in the Gupta era.
* **Key Inscriptions**:
  1. **Ashokan Schism Pillar Inscription**: 
     * Imperial decree mandating unity in the Sangha: *Ye saṃghe bhokhati bhikhu vā bhikhuni vā se odātāni dusāni saṃnaṃdhāpayitu anāvāsasi nidhāpetaviye* ("Whosoever breaks the Sangha, monk or nun, shall be made to wear white garments and be expelled to a non-monastic residence").
  2. **Vidisha Ivory Carvers Inscription**: 
     * Dedication on the South Gateway by the artisans' guild.
  3. **Satavahana Satakarni Inscription**: 
     * Confirms political sovereignty and artisan patronage under the Satavahana dynasty.
  4. **Stupa 3 Disciples Reliquary Inscriptions**:
     * Labels on steatite boxes: *Sāriputasa* and *Mahā-Mogalānasa*.

## 5. Historical & Cultural Significance
1. **Evolution of Stupa Architecture**:
   * Sanchi preserves the most intact and complete typological transition from a simple Mauryan funerary tumulus to a monumental, cosmic architectural mandalic monument in South Asia.
2. **Earliest Record of Artisan Guilds in Art**:
   * The ivory carvers' epigraph provides primary documentary proof of collective artisan guild sponsorship and the cross-medium migration of artistic techniques in ancient India.
3. **Aniconic to Iconic Transition**:
   * Sanchi showcases the full flowering of early Buddhist aniconic narrative art, immediately preceding the emergence of the anthropomorphic Buddha image at Mathura and Gandhara.
