# Methodology: Evidence Evaluation & Classification

## 1. Overview & Epistemic Stance

The credibility of Bharat Heritage Atlas depends entirely on the epistemic rigor with which cultural data is recorded. We adopt an empirical, historiographically disciplined approach:

$$\text{Claim} + \text{Evidence} + \text{Source} + \text{Uncertainty Assessment} = \text{Verified Heritage Record}$$

No claim enters the primary catalogue without attribution to verifiable archaeological, epigraphic, numismatic, textual, or peer-reviewed literature.

---

## 2. Evidence Classifications

Every claim is categorized under one of five explicit classifications:

| Classification | Definition | Application Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`STRONG_EVIDENCE`** | Corroborated physical or primary evidence. | Secure in situ archaeological stratigraphy, dated inscriptions with clear donor/reign names, numismatic hoards in context, or unanimous multi-disciplinary consensus. |
| **`GOOD_EVIDENCE`** | Highly probable finding supported by primary data with minor uncertainties. | Art-historical stylistics corroborated by paleography, unstratified archaeological discovery with clear typology, well-documented museum accession with known provenance. |
| **`SCHOLARLY_DEBATE`** | Competing peer-reviewed interpretations. | Differing scholarly readings of damaged inscriptions, conflicting dating hypotheses among reputable historians (e.g., Kanishka dating debates, Gudimallam phase dating). |
| **`TRADITIONAL_ACCOUNT`** | Religious, sthala-purana, oral, or hagiographical tradition. | Local temple traditions, medieval chronicles written centuries after events, oral folk transmissions. Explicitly flagged as traditional narrative, not verified material history. |
| **`UNVERIFIED`** | Popular claim or internet assertion lacking citation. | Assertions requiring further scholarly corroboration or debunking. Never presented as settled fact. |

---

## 3. Evidence Typology

Evidence records must identify their evidential methodology:

1. **Epigraphic**: Inscriptions on stone, copper plates, rock edicts, pillars (e.g., *Epigraphia Indica*, *Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum*).
2. **Archaeological**: Stratified excavation reports, carbon-14/TL dating, structural remains (ASI reports, university excavations).
3. **Numismatic**: Coinage, legends, mint marks, hoard contexts.
4. **Iconographic / Art Historical**: Sculptural motifs, attributes, mudras, stylistic chronologies.
5. **Architectural**: Masonry techniques, planimetric development, joinery, superstructures.
6. **Literary / Textual**: Contemporary or near-contemporary manuscripts, travelogues (Faxian, Xuanzang, Megasthenes, Al-Biruni), religious canons.
7. **Manuscript**: Epistemic analysis of surviving codices, birch bark, palm-leaf records.
8. **Scholarly Interpretation**: Analytical synthesis by established historians/archaeologists.
9. **Oral / Local Tradition**: Documented living memories, local sthala-mahatmyas.

---

## 4. Source Evaluation Hierarchy

Sources are ranked according to academic verifiability:

```text
Tier 1: Primary & Official Archaeological Records
  ├─ Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) memoirs and reports
  ├─ Epigraphia Indica & official epigraphic corpora
  └─ Official museum accession catalogues & excavation monographs

Tier 2: Peer-Reviewed Academic Literature & University Presses
  ├─ Academic journals (e.g., Journal of the Asiatic Society, Puratattva, Artibus Asiae, South Asian Studies)
  └─ Peer-reviewed monographs published by recognized academic presses

Tier 3: Reputable Academic Surveys & Secondary Syntheses
  ├─ Standard reference works (e.g., Coomaraswamy, Dani, Sircar, Nilakanta Sastri, Majumdar, Meister)

Tier 4: Discovery Aids (Non-Evidentiary)
  └─ Encyclopedias, popular articles, blogs (Used strictly to discover primary citations; NEVER cited as evidence itself)
```

---

## 5. Handling Disagreements & Uncertainty

* When leading scholars disagree on chronology or attribution, **both viewpoints are recorded** with their respective citations and evidence links.
* Dates with margins of error are preserved with explicitly defined intervals rather than artificial precision.
* The absence of evidence is transparently stated rather than filled with conjecture.
